Mato Grosso accounts for 26.6% of Brazil’s planted area in four major crops — but only 6.3% of its agricultural machinery rental establishments. Would you open a rental branch there? I would investigate first. I built Agri Rental Atlas to compare two official IBGE datasets: crop area and registered rental establishments, aligned to 2022–2024. The 2024 contrast is striking: 21.17 million planted hectares across soybeans, corn, sugar cane and cotton, alongside 267 rental establishments in Mato Grosso. But an establishment is not a machine. Planted area is not rental demand. And this rental classification excludes services with an operator. Before calling that difference an opportunity, I would check three things: • How much work is handled by farmers’ own fleets? • How much is served by contractors or fleets registered in other states? • Can utilisation and transport costs support a viable day rate? The portal turns those questions into an exploration: a state map, national comparisons and transparent planning calculators for rental operators and farm managers. Scenario inputs are kept separate from observed market data. Alt text for LinkedIn

Editorial map of Brazil using 2024 IBGE data. Mato Grosso is outlined and labelled MT. It accounts for 26.6% of Brazil’s planted area in soybeans, grain corn, sugar cane and herbaceous cotton, compared with 6.3% of agricultural machinery rental establishments without operator. Mato Grosso has 267 such establishments and 21.17 million planted hectares across the four crops. Map colours show crop-area share minus establishment share: copper for a higher crop share and teal for a higher rental share. This descriptive contrast does not prove unmet demand. Planted hectares can count the same land repeatedly, and rental serves other agricultural uses. Sources: IBGE CEMPRE table 9528, CNAE 77.31-4, and PAM table 1612.

Editorial hypothesis: a concrete geographical contrast and an operational question may prompt useful discussion with rental operators and farm managers. This is not a controlled experiment or a claim that the contrast proves unmet demand. This version was reviewed, approved and published on 20 October 2026. Future posts require separate owner review.
