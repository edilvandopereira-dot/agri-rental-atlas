Mato Grosso accounts for 26.6% of Brazil’s planted area in four major crops — but only 6.3% of its agricultural machinery rental establishments.

Would you open a rental branch there?

I would investigate first.

I built Agri Rental Atlas to compare two official IBGE datasets: crop area and registered rental establishments, aligned to 2022–2024.

The 2024 contrast is striking: 21.17 million planted hectares across soybeans, corn, sugar cane and cotton, alongside 267 rental establishments in Mato Grosso.

But an establishment is not a machine. Planted area is not rental demand. And this rental classification excludes services with an operator.

Before calling that difference an opportunity, I would check three things:
• How much work is handled by farmers’ own fleets?
• How much is served by contractors or fleets registered in other states?
• Can utilisation and transport costs support a viable day rate?

The portal turns those questions into an exploration: a state map, national comparisons and transparent planning calculators for rental operators and farm managers. Scenario inputs are kept separate from observed market data.
